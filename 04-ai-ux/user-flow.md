## Entry point

**Signal type:** New message / transcript received

Specific signal: A new incident is posted in Slack #escalations 

**What they see instantly**

“Juno is evaluating this incident…” appears right away in the Slack thread OR Recommended action given for updates in JIRA

## The flow

1. Read the Slack thread to understand what is happening.
2. Look up related tickets, Jira issues, and the company strategy.
3. Compare the information and decide how serious the issue is.
4. Make sure the recommendation is backed by real evidence before showing it.
2. “Reading the Slack thread…” → “Checking related tickets and Jira issues…” → “Comparing against RocketShip strategy…” → “Building a recommendation…”
3. Every possible P0 escalation follows the same process: read the thread → check related information → compare it to strategy → build a recommendation.

## AI moments

**Placement:** Inline & Embedded

Juno shows a simple risk card right inside the Slack thread. It tells the PM how serious the issue is, gives a short reason why, and shows where the information came from. The PM can then choose what to do next, like view the evidence, draft a Jira ticket, or dismiss it.

Juno helps the PM make the decision faster instead of making the decision for them. By showing the risk card right in Slack, the PM can quickly read it, check the proof, and pick what happens next

## Fallbacks

**Kill switch**

Juno makes a suggestion, but the human always gets the final say. Every Juno issue has an Override option so the PM can change the priority, dismiss the recommendation, or stop Juno from acting on it.

**Training signal**

What the human changes becomes a clue for how Juno should improve next time. If the PM accepts, changes, or rejects Juno’s recommendation, Juno logs that choice. Overrides are saved as a correction so Juno can make better recommendations later.

**Fail-safe**

If Juno isn’t sure, it should say so and let the human decide.
